Welcome

Patrice Baughman Borders, President
PLACEHOLDER — SAMPLE WELCOME: to be replaced by President Borders' greeting letter in the final program

Dear friends, on behalf of the Cincinnati (OH) Chapter of The Links, Incorporated, it is my joy to welcome you to Essence of Emeralds — A Night of Legacy & Light. Tonight we celebrate seventy years of friendship and service in the Queen City, and we are so glad you are here.

Over 75 years ago, a circle of Cincinnati women organized this chapter around a simple conviction: that friendship, put to work in service, can change a community. Generation after generation, the women of this chapter have kept that promise — mentoring our daughters, championing the arts, advancing health and education, and standing wherever Cincinnati has needed us most.

Tonight belongs to everyone who has carried that promise forward: our charter members and past presidents, our alumnae, our sponsors and partners, and the remarkable young women of the Links Leadership Academy for Girls, whose future is the truest measure of our legacy. We are honored to welcome our keynote speaker, Susan L. Taylor, whose life's work — lifting up our children and our community — so beautifully mirrors our own.

Thank you for your presence, your generosity, and your light. Enjoy the evening.

In friendship and service,
Patrice Baughman Borders
Patrice Baughman Borders, President

Page Six · Keynote Speaker

Susan L. Taylor

Susan L. Taylor is the founder and CEO of the National CARES Mentoring Movement, a best-selling author of four books and editor of eight others, a fourth-generation entrepreneur, and the former editor-in-chief of Essence magazine.

She joined Essence in 1970 and became editor-in-chief in 1981, later serving as editorial director and shaping Essence into a world-renowned brand with more than 8 million readers.

In 2006 she founded the National CARES Mentoring Movement as Essence CARES. With local affiliates in 58 cities, National CARES has recruited, trained, and deployed more than 250,000 mentors to schools and youth-support and mentoring organizations like Big Brothers, Big Sisters, as well as to its own culturally rooted, academic- and social-transformational initiatives.

She is the recipient of more than a dozen honorary doctorates and hundreds of awards, including the NAACP President's Award and the Henry Johnson Fisher Award from the Magazine Publishers Association, and she was the first African American woman inducted into the American Society of Magazine Editors Hall of Fame.

Page Eight · Our History

A Legacy of Service

The story of the Cincinnati (OH) Chapter of The Links, Incorporated

On March 30, 1950, a distinguished group of women answered the call to service and founded the Cincinnati (OH) Chapter.

Founding and Heritage

The chapter was chartered on March 30, 1950, by twelve visionary women: Ethel M. Taylor, Alberta E. Hanksin, Anna V. Merritt, Thema Donelson, Josephine Y. Ward, Marian Strader, Lutie Lewis, Ora Glidewell, Lottie J. Harris, Margaret Richardson, Theresa Dowell and Lucille Ridley Williams.

As the 17th chapter of The Links, Incorporated, Cincinnati (OH) has carried forward a steadfast legacy of friendship, service and excellence for more than seven decades.

Leadership and Cultural Contributions

Our chapter has been led by women who have shaped both the community and the organization. Vada W. Martin served as National President (1974–1976). Barbara J. Smith followed as National President (1980–1982) and International President (1982–1984). Link Verda R. Hazen was elected National President (2004–2006).

Cincinnati (OH) Links have enriched the cultural landscape through the arts — sponsoring concerts, festivals and collaborations that inspire and uplift.

Philanthropy and Community Impact

Our commitment to service is both historic and far reaching. The chapter established Camp Joy for children fighting cancer. We hosted the Ebony Fashion Fair, raising more than $1,000,000 for scholarships and community programs.

We have awarded countless scholarships and grants, partnering with organizations across the Greater Cincinnati area. Our global impact includes building the first Links School in Africa.

Signature Programs and Initiatives

Over the decades, our signature programs have empowered generations and addressed critical needs: College Jamboree, Discovery '70, Focus: '88 — The Black Artists, Young Achievers and the Youth Docent Program.

The Links Leadership Academy for Girls

In 2019, the Cincinnati (OH) Chapter founded The Links Leadership Academy for Girls to nurture leadership in the next generation. See Page Sixteen for the full story.

Page Sixteen · Our Signature Program

Links Leadership Academy for Girls

Where Cincinnati's daughters become Cincinnati's leaders

What It Is

The Links Leadership Academy for Girls (LLAG) is a signature, high-impact leadership development program for African American high school girls.

What Makes LLAG Special

  • Designed exclusively to uplift and empower African American girls
  • Led by accomplished professional women dedicated to mentorship and service
  • Focuses on identity, leadership voice, and confidence — not just academics
  • Connects participants to real-world civic, cultural, and professional experiences
  • Builds a lasting sisterhood rooted in accountability, belonging, and support
9 – 12Grades Served
2019Academy Founded
1 of 3National Host Sites, 2021
Nearly 100Girls at Each Career Conference

Who It's For

LLAG is designed for African American girls in grades 9–12 who are ready to lead, grow, and thrive.

Impact & Outcomes

LLAG empowers participants to become confident leaders with increased self-confidence, stronger communication skills, a clearer sense of purpose, and access to higher education and career pathways. Graduates go on to lead with excellence in their schools, communities, and beyond.

Why It's Needed

African American girls face systemic barriers, including underrepresentation and limited access to leadership development. LLAG bridges that gap — creating pathways to power, purpose, and possibility.

Mission & Vision

The Links Leadership Academy for Girls equips African American high school girls with the leadership skills, mentorship, and transformative experiences they need to recognize their power, pursue their purpose, and thrive locally, nationally, and globally.

LLAG is a signature program of the Cincinnati (OH) Chapter of The Links, Incorporated.
